# 13 — The reference's own test suite, file by file

Generated by `tools/test_inventory.py` from the pinned reference
(`references/Cataclysm-DDA/tests`). Do not edit by hand: rerun the script.

This page counts the **reference's** cases, not the port's. The port's own test count comes from
`cargo test --workspace` (the `passed` figures it prints, summed); quoting these numbers as the port's
is a mistake this port made for several rounds and corrected at round 293.

The per-file triage (re-express as a test, keep as an invariant, reproduce as
a session, or drop with a reason) lives in [parts/test-triage.md](parts/test-triage.md);
this page is the mechanical inventory it is checked against.

This is the map of what upstream already asserts, so that a ported system
can be checked against the same expectations rather than reinvented. It is
**not** an equivalence oracle — see [14-test-porting.md](14-test-porting.md)
for why, and for the rules that turn a case here into a test in the port.

## Shape of the suite

* **206 of 218 files contain test cases** - the rest are helpers - giving **1217 cases**, 83,037 lines of test code.
* **758 cases need a running game** (`g->`, an avatar, a map, a spawned NPC or monster); the other **459** exercise algorithms and data on their own.
* **69 cases carry `[slow]`** and CI runs them in a batch of their own; **{hidden} carry `[.]`**, which means CI skips them entirely.
* Upstream seeds from the clock (`--rng-seed time`) and generates random
  inputs in 27 files, so a failure is not always reproducible. A ported
  case fixes its seed — that is a strict improvement, not a deviation.

## How to read the table

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cases | `TEST_CASE` count in the file |
| Lines | size of the file |
| Needs a world | the file reaches for game state, so the case cannot run before that subsystem exists |
| Tags | upstream's own labels, the ones CI filters on |
| Port status | what the port has taken from it so far |

## The wires: what each stage reaches (audit at round 1039)

Two rounds found the same shape - **a rule that is right and nothing calls** - so this is the ledger of what the schedule actually reaches, stage by stage. A rule is
**reached** only if a stage on `environment_schedule` gets to it, directly or through one of these calls.

| Stage | Set | What it reaches |
|---|---|---|
`scheduled_stage` | Scheduled | the scheduled effects that have come due, for the avatar |
`scent_stage` | Scent | the scent trail's own advance |
`falling_stage` | Falling | falling, and the support check under it |
`fields_stage` | Fields | the field sweep, with the wind, the map facts and the refusing-gas set |
`creatures_stage` | Creatures | **`focus_stage`** (morale, pain, sleepiness, the equilibrium, the drift), then dressing, reaping the dead, and every creature's turn |
**`needs_stage`** | Creatures | the sleepiness clock **both ways** - climbing while awake, recovering while `Asleep` |
`light_stage` | Light | clearing the light and casting it again |

**Three things this ledger makes visible, and none of them are visible from a test count:**

1. ~~**The food clock is not on it.**~~ **Closed at round 1040**: `digest_stage` runs every five minutes in the creatures set, reads the creature's own cache for
   `STOMACH_SIZE_MULTIPLIER`, and hands the body its half hours. **Still open:** its vitamin and effect tables are empty until a loader installs them.
2. **`focus_stage` runs inside the creatures stage, not as its own.** The order is still declared (it is first in `creatures_stage`, before anything acts), but a reader
   looking down `add_systems` will not find it - the ledger is where that is written down.
3. ~~**Nothing reads a creature's enchantment cache into the body.**~~ **Closed at round 1040** for mutations; **still open** for items and effects, which are the cache's other
   two sources.

**A rule is not ported because it has a test; it is ported because a turn can reach it.** The three items above are the current backlog, in that order: the food clock's
stage, its enchantment inputs, and then the two rules that wait on them.

### The food chain's two open ends (round 1060)

`eating::eat_a_bite` closes the wire from a bite to the stomach, and `take_turn` now moves the clock the digest stage is gated on. Two rules the reference runs at that
moment are **not** in:

1. **the item's own effects on being eaten** - a drug's `poison`, a mutagen's mutation, a comestible's `fun` (the reference calls `consume_effects`, which runs them after

### A rule waiting for its caller: dispersion (round 1064)

`crates/sim/src/dispersion.rs` holds two rules from P4's ranged half - the **sum of sources with the total capped** (`dispersion_sources::roll`/`max`, `dispersion.cpp:8-42`) and
**the skill curve** (`dispersion_from_skill`, `ranged.cpp:2477-2497`). Both are gated, and **neither has a caller yet**: the port has no ranged attack path, so nothing
draws a shot's spread. They are **kept as rules waiting**, which is the ledger's own category, and named here so the next round that starts P4's ranged half begins at
`Character::get_weapon_dispersion` (`ranged.cpp:2500-2560`) - the rule that **composes** these two out of a weapon, a shooter and an aiming mode, and therefore the one that
needs the gun table.

What that next round needs, in order: an item's `gun_dispersion` and `gun_skill` (item data), the recoil table (`Character::recoil_total`), and the option
`GUN_DISPERSION_DIVIDER`, which the port reads from its option table.

**Round 1065 added the throwing half**, which needs nothing the port does not have: `throwing_dispersion` and `throw_dispersion_per_dodge` (`ranged.cpp:1329-1384`) are ported
and gated against the reference's arithmetic. **They still have no caller** - the next round that touches the ranged half starts at `Character::throw_item`
(`ranged.cpp:1467-1600`), which composes the spread with the item's weight and volume, the throw cost and the projectile trajectory in `Creature::projectile_attack`
(`creature.cpp:1300-1400`).

**Both of these rules carry a warning worth keeping.** The reference's comments above them are **stale**: they promise 1000 spread at skill zero and 250 at skill ten where the
code gives 2500 and 119, and 200 per dodge point at zero dexterity where the code gives 50. **A port that transcribed the comments would be wrong in every number and right in
its shape** - the worst kind of wrong, because the shape is what a reviewer checks.

**Round 1066 added the landing rule**: `crates/sim/src/ballistics.rs` holds `aim_of` (`projectile_attack_roll`, `ballistics.cpp:204-231`) and `landing` (the miss branch of
`projectile_attack`, `ballistics.cpp:314-352`). They compose the dispersion rules **into a square**, so a caller now needs only three things: an item's weight and volume, a
shooter's numbers, and a target square. **Round 1067 wrote the caller**: `crates/sim/src/throwing.rs`'s `throw_at` chains all four rules - the spread from what is thrown and who throws it, a roll of that spread,
`aim_of` for the miss, `landing` for the square - and takes the item out of the hand and puts it there. The trajectory `Creature::projectile_attack` walks (`creature.cpp:1300-1400`, `find_clear_path`) is what a shot **passes through** along the way,
which matters for what it hits, not for where it stops.

**Round 1068 wired the throw into the playtest harness**: the scenario steps `throw <item> at <x> <y>` and `expect item <item> at <x> <y>`
(`crates/cli/src/lib.rs`), and `scenarios/a_thrown_rock_lands_where_the_rules_say.txt` plays the whole chain from the real item table to a pinned square. **Round 1069 wrote what happens on landing**: `throwing::damage_of_throw` (`thrown_item_adjusted_damage`, `ranged.cpp:1411-1428`) and the blow itself, resolved through
`damage_monster` at the square the rock stopped on. **The next ranged rule with a caller waiting**: the reference's `throw_item` impact (`ranged.cpp:1500-1560`) - bash damage from weight, glass shattering,
`burst_when_filled`, `HEAVY_HIT` noise, and `TANGLE` for a net - which needs the damage rules from P4's weakpoint half and the noise store the port already has.

**Round 1072 closed it: 9,912 of 9,912 types agree**, on three rules the corpus named - melee damage is truncated to a whole number, a stated `melee_damage` replaces what it
copies, and a stated `thrown_damage` inherits its emptiness. The residue was 25 → 24 → 2 → 0.

**Round 1071 cut the residue from 25 to 24 and explained 9 of them**: a stated `melee_damage` now **replaces** what it inherits (`pressin`), and the thrown-damage fallback runs
**before** a record's own `relative`/`proportional` adjustments (which fixed the `longsword_fake` class). **What remains is 24 `_fake`/`_inferior` weapon variants whose
inherited bash damage is a few per cent high**, pinned in `crates/content/tests/content_suite/thrown_damage.rs`.

**Round 1070 opened the next slice by accident**: the thrown-damage corpus gate exposes a **melee slot inheritance bug** - 25 items that inherit `melee_damage` through
`copy-from` with a `proportional` or `relative` adjustment get the wrong base, so their bash damage (and therefore their thrown damage) is a few tenths out. The fix belongs in
`apply_melee_slots` (`crates/content/src/items.rs`), and the reference's rule is `item_factory.cpp`'s `auto_flags_reader`/`mandatory`/`optional` container semantics: a
**stated** `melee_damage` replaces what was inherited, `relative` adds to it, and `proportional` scales it - in that order, after the `copy-from` chain has been resolved.
